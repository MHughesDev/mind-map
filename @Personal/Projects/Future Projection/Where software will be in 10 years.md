Here's my best shot at where software heads over the next decade, broken into domains.

**Application / Consumer Software** Most apps become agent-driven rather than click-driven. You describe intent ("plan this trip, book it, fit my budget") and software orchestrates it across services. The standalone app icon erodes; a single conversational/agent layer sits on top of many backends. Winners are whoever owns that orchestration layer (OS makers, a few super-assistants).

**Enterprise / Business Software** SaaS shifts from "seats" to "outcomes." Instead of paying per user for a CRM, companies pay for results delivered by agents that operate the CRM. Internal tools get generated on demand rather than purchased. Big incumbents (Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow) survive by becoming the system-of-record and compliance layer that agents act through, not the UI people stare at.

**Developer Tooling / Software Creation** Code generation becomes the default; humans move up to specification, architecture, review, and verification. The scarce skill is describing systems precisely and validating correctness, not typing syntax. Expect heavy investment in testing, formal verification, and "did the AI actually do what I meant" tooling, because volume of generated code explodes.

**Infrastructure / Cloud / Compute** Inference becomes a primary cost center alongside storage and bandwidth. Specialized silicon (beyond today's GPUs) proliferates, and a lot of compute pushes to the edge and onto devices for latency and privacy. Energy availability becomes a real constraint on what software can do, making efficiency a first-class design concern again.

**Data / AI Models Themselves** Models are commodity-ish at the base layer; differentiation moves to proprietary data, fine-tuning, retrieval over private knowledge, and reliability. Smaller specialized models run locally for routine tasks, with large frontier models reserved for hard problems. "Memory" and persistent context become a core product feature, not a novelty.

**Security** This is the messiest domain. Attackers and defenders both use autonomous agents, so the tempo accelerates. Identity and provenance (proving who or what generated an action) become central. Expect more software whose entire job is monitoring, sandboxing, and constraining other software.

**Robotics / Physical / Embedded** Software finally moves convincingly into the physical world—warehouses, logistics, eldercare, home tasks—as perception and control models mature. This may be the decade's biggest surprise: the software-eats-the-world story extends to atoms, not just bits.

**My single highest-conviction bet:** the dominant interface stops being screens full of buttons and becomes intent expressed in natural language, with agents doing the execution. The biggest value (and the fiercest competition) concentrates in the orchestration layer that translates human intent into action across all the systems above.

The honest caveat: ten-year tech predictions age badly, and the wildcards—regulation, an AI capability plateau, or energy limits—could reshape any of these.
